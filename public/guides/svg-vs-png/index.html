Vector SVG vs High-Res PNG for QR Codes

How mathematical vector curves differ from pixel bitmaps, and which format guarantees zero blur on commercial packaging, posters, and digital screens.

The Core Difference: Vectors vs Bitmaps

When generating a custom QR code, choosing the right file export format is critical to preserving scanning accuracy. The two primary industry formats are Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG) and Portable Network Graphics (PNG).

SVG stores graphics as XML mathematical coordinates (points, lines, polygons, and curves). Because it is not bound to a fixed grid of pixels, an SVG can be scaled from a 1-inch business card to a 40-foot outdoor highway billboard without losing a fraction of sharpness.

PNG is a raster bitmap format consisting of a fixed grid of pixels. While excellent for digital displays, scaling a low-resolution PNG causes pixelation, antialiasing blur around module corners, and potential optical scanning failures.

Comparison Summary

Feature Vector SVG Raster PNG (up to 2048px)
Resolution Infinite (Math-based coordinates) Fixed (512px, 1024px, 2048px)
Best Application Billboards, product packaging, vinyl wraps Social media, email footers, mobile apps
Scaling Behavior Zero degradation, crisp vector edges May pixelate if scaled beyond pixel width
Software Support Adobe Illustrator, Figma, CorelDRAW, Inkscape Universal (every browser, phone, and app)
File Size Extremely lightweight (< 15 KB) Lightweight (50 KB - 300 KB)

When to Choose Vector SVG

  • Commercial Offset Printing: Print shops require vector artwork to rasterize at 300 to 2400 DPI depending on the printing plate.
  • Large-Format Signage: Banners, expo backdrops, retail storefronts, and vehicle decals.
  • Product Packaging & Labels: Bottle labels, boxes, and merchandise tags where micro-printing sharpness is essential.
  • Vector Design Integration: Importing directly into Figma, Sketch, or Adobe InDesign alongside other brand vector elements.

When to Choose High-Res PNG

  • Digital Presentation Slides: Keynote, PowerPoint, and Google Slides.
  • Web & Social Media: LinkedIn posts, Twitter/X cards, Instagram stories, and websites.
  • Email Signatures: Adding a clean QR contact card (vCard) to business email footers.
  • Immediate Photo Sharing: Sending via Slack, WhatsApp, or AirDrop for quick mobile scanning.
